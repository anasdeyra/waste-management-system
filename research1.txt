# Intelligent Waste Management System: Focused Literature Review

## 1. Research question and scope

**Research question.** To what extent can intelligent collection-tour optimization reduce total travel distance and estimated collection duration compared with a documented baseline, while satisfying the same vehicle-capacity and collection-service constraints?

The project’s optimization module plans **one single-vehicle tour at a time** from a depot, visits each selected collection point once, respects a vehicle-capacity constraint, and evaluates both Euclidean travel distance and an estimated duration comprising travel plus per-stop service time. This is closest to a **capacitated single-vehicle routing problem**: if capacity never binds, it reduces to the symmetric **Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP)**; when accumulated waste demand can force a return to the depot before all points are served, it becomes a capacity-constrained variant of the **Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP)**. The CVRP generalizes the TSP by adding vehicle capacity and, in its standard form, fleet-routing decisions; the project deliberately removes the multi-vehicle assignment dimension and evaluates route sequencing for one vehicle. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a6_3ay_3a1959_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a80-91.htm)

The literature therefore matters in three ways: it defines the routing models and their complexity, establishes construction and improvement heuristics such as nearest neighbor and 2-opt, and shows how waste-collection studies formulate constraints and evaluate routing algorithms. [pubsonline.informs](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.6.6.791)

## 3. Thematic synthesis

### Exact methods versus heuristics

The foundational literature establishes that routing problems are combinatorially difficult. Dantzig and Ramser framed the original truck-dispatching problem as assigning demands to trucks while minimizing mileage, and proposed a near-optimal linear-programming-based procedure rather than claiming universally scalable exact optimization.  Later CVRP scholarship explicitly treats the CVRP as an extension of the TSP and surveys exact algorithms, but those methods are generally intended to certify optimal or near-optimal solutions under controlled conditions rather than serve as the default approach for every practical routing application. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a6_3ay_3a1959_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a80-91.htm)

For this project, exact optimization is not the appropriate primary comparator: the system plans one tour at a time, uses synthetic Euclidean scenarios, and seeks a reproducible engineering evaluation rather than a proof of optimality. The literature supports heuristic route construction and local improvement as legitimate approaches, provided their outputs are labeled heuristic and compared under identical constraints. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1965.tb04146.x)

### Nearest-neighbor construction

Nearest-neighbor construction is a greedy sequencing rule: from the current location, choose the next feasible collection point with the shortest distance, subject to remaining capacity. Its main strength is simplicity, low implementation complexity, and fast production of an initial feasible tour. The broader heuristic-routing literature places such constructive methods alongside savings-based procedures such as Clarke and Wright’s method, which likewise aims to produce good feasible routes rapidly rather than guarantee global optimality. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmoropre/v_3a12_3ay_3a1964_3ai_3a4_3ap_3a568-581.htm)

Its known weakness is **myopia**: a locally shortest next hop can create a poor return path to the depot or leave awkwardly located points for later in the route. This is an established general limitation of greedy construction rather than a waste-collection-specific result. In a capacity-constrained setting, feasibility also depends on demand: the nearest feasible point may not be admissible if adding its waste load would exceed vehicle capacity. The project’s capacity-aware nearest-neighbor variant therefore needs an explicit rule for skipping infeasible points and returning to the depot when necessary.

### 2-opt and local search

Croes’s TSP method introduced a mechanized improvement approach associated with exchanging tour edges, and Lin subsequently studied heuristic local search that produces precisely characterized locally optimal tours for larger TSPs.  In a 2-opt move, two non-adjacent edges are removed and reconnected in the alternative way; the move is accepted when it shortens the tour. This directly addresses a weakness of nearest-neighbor routes, such as crossing edges or long detours created by greedy insertion. [pubsonline.informs](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.6.6.791)

However, Lin’s results make the central limitation explicit: local search yields **local optima**, not proof of a global optimum. Repeated runs from different initial tours can increase the chance of finding better local optima, but do not eliminate the possibility that a better tour exists.  Thus, “nearest neighbor plus 2-opt” should be described as a deterministic improvement heuristic when it starts from one fixed nearest-neighbor tour, not as a globally optimal routing method. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1965.tb04146.x)

### Capacity and service constraints

Capacity is not incidental in waste collection. Dantzig and Ramser’s original model includes station demands and truck capacities, while Clarke and Wright explicitly address vehicles of varying capacities departing from a central depot.  In the project, each collection point has a waste load, the vehicle has a fixed capacity, and a feasible tour must ensure that the cumulative load between depot returns remains within capacity. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a6_3ay_3a1959_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a80-91.htm)

The waste-collection literature often extends this basic structure. Babaee Tirkolaee et al. model multi-trip vehicles, time windows, priorities, capacity, and vehicle usage-time limits, with an objective combining traversal cost, vehicle employment cost, and time-window penalties.  Their model is substantially richer than the project’s: it allows multiple trips, uses time windows, and optimizes monetary cost rather than distance and estimated duration alone. This difference is important because a method that performs well under their constraints cannot be assumed to dominate simpler heuristics under the project’s single-trip, capacity-only constraints. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30761957/)

### Waste-collection evaluation practice

Waste-collection routing studies commonly use real municipal cases, GIS-derived distances, demand data, and sometimes commercial solvers as reference points. Babaee Tirkolaee et al., for example, combine a real Iranian case study with CPLEX comparison and report computation time alongside solution quality.  Reviews by Sulemana et al. and Han and Ponce-Cueto emphasize that methods, data availability, and implementation contexts vary considerably across studies. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/4586376)

Recent systematic work similarly organizes the field by model variants, objectives, constraints, solution techniques, and applications, rather than endorsing one universal algorithm.  This supports the project’s decision to use **controlled synthetic scenarios**: they remove road-network, traffic, sensor, and demand-uncertainty confounders, making the effect of route sequencing easier to attribute. But it also means the results are algorithmic evidence on Euclidean test cases, not evidence about operational savings in a particular Tunisian municipality or any real collection system. [growingscience](https://www.growingscience.com/ijiec/Vol16/IJIEC_2024_31.pdf)

### Improvement versus computational cost

The literature distinguishes solution quality from computation time. Croes emphasized a mechanizable and relatively fast TSP improvement procedure, while Lin reported that local-search heuristics could produce locally optimal solutions for comparatively large TSPs in short time on the hardware of the period.  More recent waste-collection work often uses metaheuristics such as simulated annealing when models include multiple trips, time windows, and uncertainty. [pubsonline.informs](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.6.6.791)

For the project, 2-opt adds computation beyond nearest neighbor because it repeatedly evaluates candidate edge exchanges until no improving move remains. The appropriate claim is therefore conditional: 2-opt may reduce distance and estimated duration relative to nearest neighbor, but it may require more computation. The project should report runtime as well as distance and duration, rather than presenting improvement as free.

## 4. Research gap and project positioning

### What the literature establishes

The literature establishes that:

- Waste collection is a standard application domain for vehicle-routing models involving depots, demand-bearing collection points, capacities, and route-cost objectives. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a6_3ay_3a1959_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a80-91.htm)
- The TSP is the unconstrained sequencing core, while the CVRP adds capacity and fleet considerations; the project is a simplified single-vehicle, capacity-constrained version. [cris.unibo](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/527551)
- Greedy construction heuristics and local-search improvement are established, computationally practical alternatives to exact optimization. [pubsonline.informs](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.6.6.791)
- Local-search solutions are locally optimal, not globally optimal; repeated starts or more powerful metaheuristics may be needed for stronger solution quality. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1965.tb04146.x)
- Waste-collection studies frequently include richer constraints—multiple trips, time windows, uncertain demand, emissions, or multi-objective costs—than the project’s model. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30761957/)

### What remains difficult

Existing research does not provide a single, universally accepted answer to how much a simple nearest-neighbor-plus-2-opt pipeline improves over input-order routing for **small synthetic single-vehicle waste-collection scenarios**. Much waste-collection work emphasizes real-world case studies, GIS data, richer constraints, or metaheuristics; those results are valuable but not directly transferable to the project’s controlled Euclidean setting. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/4586376)

There is also a persistent evaluation gap: published improvements often depend on instance geometry, demand distribution, depot placement, capacity tightness, service-time assumptions, and the chosen baseline. A review can identify common methods and constraints, but it cannot substitute for a controlled experiment using identical scenarios, seeds, constraints, and metrics. [growingscience](https://www.growingscience.com/ijiec/Vol16/IJIEC_2024_31.pdf)

### What the prototype can borrow

The project can legitimately borrow:

- The TSP/CVRP distinction when defining its routing model. [cris.unibo](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/527551)
- Capacity-aware feasibility checking during tour construction. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a6_3ay_3a1959_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a80-91.htm)
- Nearest-neighbor construction as a simple, reproducible baseline-improvement method. [econpapers.repec](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmoropre/v_3a12_3ay_3a1964_3ai_3a4_3ap_3a568-581.htm)
- 2-opt as a local-search improvement step, with the explicit caveat that it produces local optima. [pubsonline.informs](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.6.6.791)
- The practice of reporting both solution quality and computational effort. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1965.tb04146.x)
- The use of clearly defined constraints and metrics before claiming improvement. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30761957/)

### Why the three-way comparison is reasonable but limited

Comparing **input order**, **nearest neighbor**, and **nearest neighbor plus 2-opt** on identical seeded scenarios is reasonable because it isolates the marginal value of (a) intelligent sequencing and (b) local-search improvement while holding depot location, point coordinates, demands, capacity, service times, and feasibility rules constant. It also avoids the complexity of multi-vehicle assignment, which the project explicitly excludes.

Its limitations are equally clear:

- Input-order routing is a weak, non-optimized baseline; improvement over it demonstrates the value of sequencing, not competitiveness with professional routing systems.
- Nearest neighbor is only one constructive heuristic; savings-based construction, insertion heuristics, or metaheuristics may perform differently.
- 2-opt is a basic local-search operator; it does not represent the state of the art in waste-collection routing.
- Euclidean distances ignore real road-network topology, one-way streets, traffic, elevation, vehicle dynamics, and operational disruptions.
- A single vehicle and one tour at a time cannot support conclusions about fleet-level coordination, workload balancing, or multi-vehicle capacity allocation.
- Synthetic scenarios support internal validity and reproducibility, not external validity for a real municipality.

### Supported and unsupported conclusions

After running the experiments, the prototype will be able to support conclusions of this form: **on the specified seeded synthetic scenarios, nearest-neighbor construction reduced or did not reduce total distance and estimated duration relative to input-order routing, while preserving the same capacity and service constraints; adding 2-opt further reduced or did not reduce these metrics, at a reported additional computational cost.**

It will **not** be able to support claims that:

- Nearest neighbor plus 2-opt is globally optimal.
- The observed percentage improvement will generalize to Tunisian streets, real waste quantities, traffic, or another city.
- The method outperforms modern commercial routing engines, exact CVRP solvers, or advanced metaheuristics.
- The results demonstrate fleet-level savings or benefits from IoT sensing, real-time GPS, or dynamic re-routing.
- Duration improvements are operationally valid unless the service-time model and travel-time assumptions are validated against real operations.
